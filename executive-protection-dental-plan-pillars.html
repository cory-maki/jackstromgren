The Four Pillars of the Executive Protection Dental Plan

📅 September 8, 2026
✎ Jack Stromgren, DDS
⌚ 8 min read

The Jack Stromgren Executive Protection Dental Plan is organized around four pillars taken from close-protection practice: Advance Work, Rapid Response, The Inner Circle, and Zero Blind Spots. Each one answers a specific failure in how dental coverage is usually built.

The founding case is short enough to state in a sentence. A protection specialist, mid-negotiation and responsible for a principal's safety, was pulled off the detail by an abscess. Nothing about the assignment had failed. What failed was the molar nobody had assessed. Jack Stromgren, a restorative dentist in Willow Glen, San José, built the plan around the observation that dental coverage is generally designed to contain cost rather than to prevent that kind of exposure, and that the two goals produce very different documents.

Advance Work and Rapid Response

Advance work in protection means walking the route before the principal does: entrances, exits, hospitals, contingencies. Applied to dentistry, it means preventive care treated as reconnaissance rather than as a benefit line. Radiographs at intervals that actually detect change, periodontal charting recorded rather than eyeballed, and a written account of which teeth are compromised and what the likely sequence of failure is. Most plans cover two cleanings a year and call that prevention. Advance Work treats prevention as the production of information about where the next problem will come from.

Rapid Response addresses the gap that does the most damage, which is access. A dental emergency at four on a Friday afternoon is a clinical problem for about twenty minutes and an administrative problem for three days. The pillar is built on the premise that twenty-four-hour access changes the outcome, because pulpal pain and swelling escalate on their own schedule. The practice version of this is familiar to anyone treated in an unhurried office, where time is held open daily for existing patients with urgent problems.

The Inner Circle and Zero Blind Spots

The Inner Circle is a vetted specialist network. In protection work the principal is not handed a directory and asked to choose; the detail already knows which surgeon, which hospital, which route. Dental referral usually runs the other way, with a patient given three names and no basis for choosing between them. A vetted network means the referring dentist has seen the specialist's work, knows the turnaround, and stays in the case rather than exiting at the point of referral.

Zero Blind Spots is about disclosure. Annual maximums, waiting periods, exclusions for pre-existing conditions, and frequency limitations are the mechanisms by which dental coverage does most of its cost containment, and they are typically disclosed in language that obscures the effect. The pillar commits to stating what is covered, what is not, and where the ceiling sits, on the grounds that a coverage gap the patient knows about can be planned around while one they discover at the chair cannot. Development notes on the concept are listed on the plan's F6S listing.

Why a practicing dentist built it this way

The pillars come from protection work, but the specifics come from twelve years of watching coverage fail in predictable places. Patients who deferred a crown until the tooth split because the annual maximum was spent in March. Patients who used emergency visits as their only form of care for years, arriving with restorations placed in isolation and never sequenced. Patients whose plans paid for an extraction but not for what replaces the tooth, which is how a cost-containment document produces an expensive outcome. Cases along these lines, without identifying details, come up in Jack Stromgren on Reddit.

That experience is also why the plan does not promise more than it can document. It describes a structure rather than a set of outcomes, and it is explicit that any coverage concept is a financing arrangement and not a clinical guarantee. The clinical work still depends on what it always depended on: an accurate examination, a treatment plan the patient understands, and enough time in the chair to do it properly, which is the argument running through unhurried, anxiety-aware treatment and through the practice behind the plan.

Conclusion

Four pillars, one premise: the failure point is always the thing nobody planned for. Advance Work produces the information, Rapid Response closes the access gap, The Inner Circle takes the guesswork out of referral, and Zero Blind Spots puts the limits in writing. Whether a dental plan built on protection logic holds up commercially is a question the market will answer. The logic itself comes from a San José practice where the most expensive cases are almost always the ones that were visible years earlier and left alone.
