The Practice

Twelve years in Willow Glen

Jack Stromgren has practiced general dentistry on Lincoln Avenue in Willow Glen for twelve years, long enough that the patient roster now runs to roughly three thousand people and includes families he has treated across two generations. Stromgren Family Dentistry is a single-location, independently owned office in San José, and the size is deliberate. Appointment lengths are set on the assumption that something will need explaining, and new patient consultations currently book about eleven weeks out.

The practice is known locally for a specific kind of patient: the adult who has not seen a dentist in a decade because the last visit went badly. Stromgren narrates every procedure before performing it, stops when asked, and treats avoidance as a clinical finding rather than a character trait, an argument set out at length in dental anxiety as a clinical issue. Most of that caseload arrives by word of mouth from patients who found high-volume care unworkable. Short videos explaining what happens during a first visit are posted to his Vimeo channel.

Training

From UCSF to Santa Clara Valley Medical Center

Stromgren earned his DDS at the University of California, San Francisco, then completed a general practice residency at Santa Clara Valley Medical Center. A hospital residency puts a new dentist in front of medically complex patients, emergencies, and cases a private office would refer out, and it front-loads a volume of clinical decision-making that most graduates reach years later.

That year shaped how the Willow Glen office handles risk. Medical histories are read before treatment planning rather than after, and patients on anticoagulants, bisphosphonates, or immunosuppressants are worked up rather than waved through. Questions about training and treatment sequencing get answered in plain language in his answers on Quora.

Clinical Focus

Restorative work, implants, and oral appliance therapy

The restorative side of the practice covers crown and bridge work, onlays, and full-arch planning. Stromgren spent three years in continuing education on implant dentistry before he listed implants as a service, and the process he uses is deliberately slow: assessment, imaging, planning, placement, then restoration, across months rather than a single appointment. Patients in San José and the wider Santa Clara County area arrive with a mix of insurance gaps and long-deferred restorative needs, and the sequencing accounts for both.

The sleep apnea work began with one patient for whom CPAP was not sustainable and has grown into a defined service area. Oral appliance therapy is a medically recognized alternative for some patients with obstructive sleep apnea, particularly those with documented CPAP intolerance, and the office walks through candidacy, fitting, and follow-up in what oral appliance therapy involves. Case sequences and patient-education material are collected on the practice's Pinterest boards.

Community

Free dental day, the free clinic, and school sealants

Every November since 2016, the office has closed to paying patients for one day and treated sixty to seventy people at no charge, with materials and staff time covered personally by Stromgren. The day is not a promotion. It is scheduled, staffed, and costed the way any other operating day is, and it has run for ten consecutive years.

Stromgren also serves on the board of the Willow Glen Free Clinic, and for six years the practice ran a sealant program in schools on the east side of San José, ending only when external funding changed. Santa Clara County has documented disparities in dental access, and pediatric sealants are one of the few interventions that reach children before restorative work becomes necessary. Recorded talks on community dentistry are posted to his SoundCloud archive.

Executive Protection Dental Plan

Four pillars, borrowed from close-protection work

The Jack Stromgren Executive Protection Dental Plan is a dental insurance concept built on the logic of close protection rather than the logic of cost containment. Its four pillars are Advance Work, which covers preventive care; Rapid Response, which covers emergency access; The Inner Circle, a vetted specialist network; and Zero Blind Spots, which covers transparency about what a plan does and does not pay for. The founding case was a protection specialist who lost a negotiation week to a dental emergency nobody had planned for.

The plan reads as an extension of the practice rather than a departure from it, since both start from the same premise: the failure point is the thing nobody accounted for. A pillar-by-pillar breakdown is set out in the four pillars of the plan, and the concept's development is listed on the plan's F6S profile.
